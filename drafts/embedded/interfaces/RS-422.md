**RS-422 (Recommended Standard 422)** is a hardware interface for distant,
serial communication. It uses twisted pairs with differential signaling. It's
usually used in full-duplex mode for point-to-point communications.

**Full-duplex or Half-duplex:**  
The specification only defines one twisted pair and doesn't assign it to any
function. So, RS-422 might be used in half-duplex mode, but often it's used in
full-duplex mode. ICs' manufactures use two twisted pairs to achieve it. RS-422
also might have additional receivers (unidirectional multidrop bus) on any data
path.

# Physical characteristics
The interface doesn't define a maximum cable length or work speeds. But usually
it uses:
- Cables are less than 1200m long
- Baud rate may vary up to 10MBd.

RS-422 interface can transmit data up to 10 Mbit/s or up to 1,200 meters with
lower rates (like 100 Kbit/s). Speed and length are inversely proportional to
each other.

Voltage:
Term    | Data signals | Differential voltage
--      | --           | --
Space   | 0            | +2V - +6V
Mark    | 1            | -6V - -2V
Invalid | -            | -2V - +2V

# Connections
RS-422 ICs Usually have TX+, TX-, RX+, RX- and GND lines.

Manufactures might additionally add any subset of RS-232 signals (DCD, DTR, DSR,
RTS, CTS), where every signal is two pins that are used for a twisted pair.

- Connectors are usually DE-9 or terminal blocks.
- Pinouts are manufacture-specific.
