**RS-232 (Recommended Standard 232)** is a hardware interface for serial
communication. It provides a full-duplex point-to-point bits transmission, flow
control and a devices' status notification.

The interface defines:
- Electrical characteristics
- Signal meanings
- Connector sizes
- Pinouts

The devices that implement RS-232 are called RS-232 drivers. They are usually
used together with UART ICs in order to communicate. UART ICs control data flow,
build frames and convert data into serial signals. And RS-232 drivers, in turn,
convert the logic levels of the UART to the voltage levels specified by RS-232.

Example of transmission by an UART IC and RS-232 driver:
- UART IC:
  - Receives data in parallel from a microcontroller.
  - Builds data frames.
  - Outputs data frames in serial format to the RS-232 driver.
- RS-232 driver IC:
  - Receives data in serial format.
  - Converts logic level signals into RS-232 signals.
  - Outputs RS-232 signals over the wires.


# Physical characteristics

- Cables:
  - The interface defines only maximum capacitance that a complaint drive
    circuit must tolerate.
  - Cables mostly are less than 15m long
- Connectors:
  - The interface defines two standard connectors: DB-25 and DE-9.
- Speeds:
  - The interface doesn't define specific speeds.
  - The most common speeds are: 4800, 9600, 19200, 38400, 57600, 115200 bps.

Voltage:
Term    | Data signals | Control signals | Voltage
--      | --           | --              | --
Space   | 0            | Asserted        | +3V - +15V
Mark    | 1            | Deasserted      | -15V - -3V
Invalid | -            | -               | -3V - +3V


# DE-9 Connector
- **DE-9p** (p - plug) denotes a DTE device.
- **DE-9s** (s - socket) denotes a DCE device.

Abbreviation | Denotation          | Pin | DTE perspective | Description
--           | --                  | --  | --              | --
DCD          | Data Carrier Detect | 1   | In              | The DCE notifies that it's connected to another DCE device.
RxD          | Receive Data        | 2   | In              | Carries data from the DCE to the DTE.
TxD          | Transmit Data       | 3   | Out             | Carries data from the DTE to the DCE.
DTR          | Data Terminal Ready | 4   | Out             | The DTE notifies that it's initialized and ready to work.
GND          | Common Ground       | 5   | Common          | Zero voltage reference.
DSR          | Data Set Ready      | 6   | In              | The DCE notifies that it's initialized and ready to work.
RTS (RTR)    | Request To Send     | 7   | Out             | The DTE requests the DCE for a permission to send data.
CTS          | Clear To Send       | 8   | In              | The DCE allows the DTE to send data.
RI           | Ring Indicator      | 9   | In              | The DCE notifies the DTE about the incomming call.

> **NOTE:** RTS and CTS were initially used for half-duplex communication. Now
RTS has another meaning RTR (Ready To Receive).

Modern flow control:
- CTS is used to stop transmission from DTE.
- RTR (RTS) is used to stop transmission from DCE.

Often used only **3-wire** or **5-wire** connections:
  - 3-wire connections use RxD, TxD and GND lines.
  - 5-wire connections use additionally RTR and CTS.


# Null modem
A null modem is an adapter that allows communication between DTEs or between
DCEs. RS-232 is an asymmetric interface and wasn't designed for such
communications. A null modem just crosslinks the connections without any logic
on its own.

There is also a null mode cable that allows communication between DTEs.
